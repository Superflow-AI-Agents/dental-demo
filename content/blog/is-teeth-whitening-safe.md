# Is teeth whitening safe?

<p class="byline">By <a href="/team/dr-helen-whitfield">Dr. Helen Whitfield, DDS</a> · September 29, 2026</p>

<img src="/media/blog/is-teeth-whitening-safe.jpg" alt="A pair of clear whitening trays in an open case next to a shade guide on a dental counter" width="800" height="500">

Whitening is one of the most popular things we do, and one of the questions I am asked most often is whether it damages your teeth. For most healthy adults, peroxide whitening used as directed is safe. It does not thin or soften enamel in any meaningful way. But it is not right for everyone, and a few common shortcuts can do real harm.

## How whitening works

Professional whitening uses hydrogen peroxide or carbamide peroxide, which breaks down into hydrogen peroxide. The peroxide passes through the enamel and breaks apart the stain molecules inside the tooth, so they reflect less color. It is a chemical process, not a scrubbing one, so the tooth surface is not worn away.

Whitening works best on yellow and brown stains from coffee, tea, red wine, tobacco and aging. Gray stains, such as those from certain medications taken in childhood, respond more slowly and less predictably.

## The real side effects

The two side effects we see are common, temporary and manageable.

- **Tooth sensitivity.** Many people feel short zings during or for a day or two after whitening. It fades once you stop or take a break.
- **Gum irritation.** If gel touches the gums, they can turn white or feel sore for a few hours. Well-fitted trays and careful application keep gel where it belongs.

To keep these mild, we may suggest a toothpaste for sensitive teeth for two weeks before you start, shorter sessions, or a day off between sessions.

## What whitening cannot do

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>Whitening only works on natural teeth.</strong> Crowns, veneers, bonding and tooth-colored fillings will not change color. If you have visible dental work in your smile, plan for it before you whiten, so the new shade matches.</p>
</div>

Whitening also cannot fix stains caused by decay or a tooth that has darkened after an injury. Those need a different approach, which is one reason we always check your teeth first.

## Who should wait or skip whitening

Whitening is not a good idea right now if you:

- Have untreated cavities or a cracked tooth. Peroxide reaching the inner tooth can cause significant pain.
- Have gum disease or receding gums with exposed roots. Roots do not whiten and can become very sensitive.
- Are pregnant or breastfeeding. There is no strong evidence of harm, but it is a cosmetic treatment and we suggest waiting.
- Are a young teen whose teeth are still maturing. We usually suggest waiting until the late teens.
- Already have significant sensitivity.

## Whitening options compared

### In-office whitening

A higher-strength gel applied by your dental team while your gums are protected. You see a noticeable change in a single visit. In-office whitening at our practice is $349.

### Take-home trays

We make clear trays from a scan of your teeth so they fit snugly, and give you a lower-strength gel to wear for a set time each day. Results build over about two weeks. Custom take-home whitening trays are $199, and you can keep the trays for touch-ups later.

### Store-bought kits

Over-the-counter strips and gels contain lower concentrations and can work for mild stains. The main problems are one-size trays that let gel leak onto the gums, and whitening teeth that have untreated problems underneath.

## What to avoid

Some popular home methods are not safe for enamel:

<ul class="check-list">
<li>Skip abrasive powders, including charcoal, which can wear down enamel and make teeth look more yellow over time.</li>
<li>Do not rub lemon juice, vinegar or fruit acids on your teeth. Acid dissolves enamel.</li>
<li>Do not scrub with baking soda every day or use more than the directions say.</li>
<li>Avoid kits sold without clear ingredient labels or with lights promising instant results.</li>
<li>Do not exceed the wear time on any whitening product. More is not better.</li>
</ul>

## Keeping your results

Results usually last one to three years, depending on what you eat and drink. Rinse with water after coffee, tea or red wine, keep up regular cleanings and use your trays for a short touch-up when you notice the shade slipping.
