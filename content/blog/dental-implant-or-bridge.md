# Dental implant or bridge: how to choose

<p class="byline">By <a href="/team/dr-samuel-brandt">Dr. Samuel Brandt, DDS</a> · August 18, 2026</p>

<img src="/media/blog/dental-implant-or-bridge.jpg" alt="A dental model showing an implant post beside a three-tooth bridge on a clinic counter" width="800" height="500">

When a tooth is lost or has to come out, the gap is more than a cosmetic issue. Neighboring teeth can slowly drift or tip into the space, the opposite tooth can grow longer into it, and chewing can shift to the other side. For a single missing tooth, the two most common fixes are a dental implant and a fixed bridge. Both work well. The right one depends on your mouth, your health and your priorities.

## How each one works

### Dental implant

An implant is a small titanium post placed in the jawbone where the root used to be. Over the next few months, the bone grows tightly around it. Once it has healed, we attach a connector and a custom crown on top. The finished tooth stands on its own and does not rely on the teeth beside it.

### Fixed bridge

A traditional bridge spans the gap. We shape the teeth on either side of the space and cover them with crowns. A replacement tooth is joined between those two crowns, and the whole piece is cemented in place as one unit. It is not removable.

## When an implant tends to be the better choice

- **The neighboring teeth are healthy.** If they have no fillings or crowns, trimming them down for a bridge removes healthy enamel that cannot be put back.
- **You want the most tooth-like result.** An implant is cleaned and feels much like a natural tooth.
- **You are thinking long term.** Implants have a high success rate and do not depend on the health of other teeth.
- **Several teeth are missing.** Implants can also support a bridge or a denture, so fewer posts can replace more teeth.

## When a bridge tends to be the better choice

- **The neighboring teeth already need crowns.** If they have large fillings or cracks, crowning them serves two purposes at once.
- **You want it done quickly,** or you would rather avoid surgery.
- **There is not enough bone** for an implant and you prefer not to have a bone graft.
- **Health factors make implant healing less predictable,** such as heavy smoking, poorly controlled diabetes, some medications that affect bone, or recent radiation to the jaw.

## Questions to ask yourself

<ul class="check-list">
<li>How healthy are the teeth on each side of the gap?</li>
<li>How do I feel about a minor surgical procedure?</li>
<li>Do I need the tooth replaced by a certain date?</li>
<li>How much time will I realistically spend cleaning under a bridge each day?</li>
<li>How long do I want this repair to last before I think about it again?</li>
</ul>

## What about cost?

Upfront, an implant usually costs more than a bridge. Over many years the gap can narrow, because a bridge may need replacing and can affect the health of the supporting teeth. Insurance coverage varies a lot between plans. We give you a written plan with costs for each option before you decide, so you can compare them on paper. Our [insurance and financing](/insurance-and-financing) page explains payment options.

## How we help you decide

At a consultation, we examine the gap and the neighboring teeth, and take the images we need to measure the bone if an implant is a possibility. Then we walk through both options, including the timeline, the costs and anything that might affect the outcome for you. There is no pressure to decide that day.
