## What a filling is, and when you need one

A cavity is a spot where acid from plaque bacteria has dissolved part of the tooth. Once decay breaks through the hard outer enamel, it spreads faster in the softer layer underneath. It will not heal on its own.

A filling removes the decay and seals the space so bacteria cannot get back in. We use tooth-colored composite, a resin filled with fine glass particles. It bonds to the tooth, so we can keep more of your natural tooth than older metal fillings needed.

Very early spots that have not broken through the enamel can sometimes be reversed with fluoride and better cleaning at home. When that is an option, we will tell you and check the spot again at your next visit instead of drilling.

## What to expect at your appointment

Most fillings take 30 to 60 minutes, depending on the size and how many surfaces of the tooth are involved.

1. **Numbing.** We apply numbing gel first, then a local anesthetic. Small, shallow fillings sometimes need no injection at all. You decide.
2. **Removing decay.** We clean out the soft, damaged part of the tooth and shape the space so the filling holds well.
3. **Bonding and layering.** We treat the surface so the composite sticks, then place it in thin layers, hardening each one with a blue curing light.
4. **Shaping and polishing.** We shape the filling to fit your bite, check it with a thin strip of marking paper, and polish it smooth.

You can watch, listen to music, or ask us to talk you through each step. Raise a hand any time you want a break.

## After your filling

Your lip and cheek may stay numb for two to three hours. Wait until the feeling comes back before you eat, so you do not bite your cheek. Composite is fully hardened when you leave, so there is no waiting period for the filling itself.

Mild sensitivity to cold or to biting is common for a week or two, especially with deeper fillings. It should fade steadily. Call us if it gets worse instead of better, or if your bite feels high or uneven. A quick adjustment usually fixes that in a few minutes.

A well-placed composite filling often lasts ten years or more. Brushing twice a day, flossing, and keeping up with [cleanings and exams](/services/cleanings-and-exams) give it the best chance.

## Cost and insurance

The cost of a filling depends on its size and how many surfaces of the tooth it covers. Most PPO plans cover fillings as basic care, usually for a share of the cost after your deductible. We check your benefits and give you a written estimate before we start, so there are no surprises. See [insurance and financing](/insurance-and-financing) for payment options.

## Common questions

<details class="faq">
<summary>Can you replace my old silver fillings?</summary>
<p>Yes, but we only suggest it when an old filling is cracked, leaking, or has decay around it. Removing a sound filling takes away healthy tooth, so if yours are doing their job, we will usually leave them alone.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Will I feel the filling being placed?</summary>
<p>You will feel pressure and vibration, but not sharp pain. If anything feels sharp, tell us and we will give you more anesthetic before we go on.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Why does my tooth hurt more after a filling than before?</summary>
<p>Deep cavities sit close to the nerve, and the tooth can be irritated for a little while after treatment. Mild sensitivity that fades is normal. Pain that lingers, throbs, or keeps you awake is not, so call us.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Do kids get the same fillings?</summary>
<p>Yes. Baby teeth hold space for adult teeth, so we fix cavities in them too. See <a href="/services/kids-dentistry">kids dentistry</a> for how we make those visits easier.</p>
</details>

## Think you have a cavity?

Sensitivity to sweets or cold, a rough spot, or food catching between teeth are all worth a look. Small cavities are quicker and cheaper to fix.
