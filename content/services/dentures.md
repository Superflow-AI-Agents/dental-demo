## Types of dentures

A denture is a removable set of replacement teeth on a gum-colored base. Which type suits you depends on how many teeth you have left and how much hold you want.

### Full dentures

For an upper or lower jaw with no teeth left. An upper denture covers the roof of the mouth, which helps it stay in place by suction. A lower denture sits on the ridge of the jaw and usually moves more.

### Partial dentures

For when you still have some healthy teeth. A partial fills the gaps and clips onto nearby teeth with small clasps. It also stops your remaining teeth from drifting into the spaces.

### Implant-supported dentures

A full denture that snaps onto two or more [dental implants](/services/dental-implants). It does not slide when you eat or talk, and a lower denture held this way is a big step up in comfort for most people.

### Immediate dentures

Made before your remaining teeth are removed, and placed the same day, so you are never without teeth. Your gums shrink as they heal, so an immediate denture needs a reline or replacement after a few months.

## What to expect, step by step

Making a well-fitting denture takes four to six visits over a few weeks.

1. **Exam and planning.** We check your gums, bone and any remaining teeth, and talk about the options that fit your mouth and budget.
2. **Impressions.** We take detailed impressions of your gums so the base fits closely.
3. **Bite and tooth selection.** We record how your jaws meet, and choose the shade, shape and size of the teeth with you.
4. **Try-in.** You try a wax version with the real teeth set in it. You can see the look, check the bite, and ask for changes before it is finished.
5. **Delivery and adjustments.** We fit the final denture and show you how to put it in, take it out, and clean it. Then we see you again within a few days to ease any sore spots.

## Getting used to your dentures

The first few weeks take practice. Your mouth may make more saliva, speech may feel odd for some words, and eating takes care. Start with soft food cut into small pieces, chew on both sides at once, and read aloud to get used to speaking. Most people feel settled within a month.

Sore spots are common early on and are easy to fix. Call us instead of putting up with them.

## Caring for dentures

<ul class="check-list">
<li>Take them out and rinse after meals</li>
<li>Brush them daily with a soft brush and a denture cleaner, not regular toothpaste, which scratches</li>
<li>Take them out at night and keep them in water or a soaking solution so they do not dry out</li>
<li>Brush your gums, tongue and any natural teeth every day</li>
<li>Come in once a year so we can check the fit and screen your mouth for problems</li>
</ul>

## Common questions

<details class="faq">
<summary>How long do dentures last?</summary>
<p>Most dentures last five to ten years. Your gums and jawbone slowly change shape over time, so even well-made dentures need a reline every few years and eventual replacement. Signs it is time include looseness, new sore spots, clicking, or worn-down teeth.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Will I be able to eat normally?</summary>
<p>Most people go back to a fairly normal diet once they adjust. Very hard, sticky or chewy foods, like tough meat, corn on the cob or caramel, stay harder with dentures. Implant-supported dentures make a big difference here.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Do I need denture adhesive?</summary>
<p>A well-fitting upper denture often needs none. Adhesive can help a lower denture or give extra confidence. If you find you need more and more of it, the denture is probably loose and needs a reline.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Can I sleep in my dentures?</summary>
<p>We recommend taking them out at night. It gives your gums a rest, lowers the risk of fungal infection under the denture, and lets you clean them properly.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Can you fix a broken denture?</summary>
<p>Often, yes. Cracks, a broken tooth, or a snapped clasp can usually be repaired, sometimes the same day. Bring all the pieces and do not try to glue it at home, as household glue can damage it for good.</p>
</details>
