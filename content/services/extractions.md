## When a tooth needs to come out

Removing a tooth is usually the last option, not the first. A [root canal](/services/root-canals) or a [crown](/services/crowns-and-bridges) can save many teeth that look hopeless at first. We may recommend an extraction when:

<ul class="check-list">
<li>A tooth is broken below the gumline or too decayed to repair</li>
<li>Advanced gum disease has loosened it beyond saving</li>
<li>A wisdom tooth is stuck, partly through the gum, or keeps getting infected</li>
<li>Teeth are crowded and need space for orthodontic treatment</li>
<li>A baby tooth has not fallen out and is blocking the adult tooth</li>
</ul>

We will show you the x-ray, explain why we think the tooth should go, and tell you what happens if you wait. The choice is yours.

## Simple and surgical extractions

A **simple extraction** is for a tooth you can see above the gum. After numbing, we loosen the tooth with an instrument called an elevator and lift it out with forceps. Most take a few minutes once you are numb.

A **surgical extraction** is for a tooth that is broken off at the gum or has not come in fully, which is common with wisdom teeth. We make a small opening in the gum, sometimes remove a little bone or divide the tooth into pieces, and then close the area with a few stitches. Many stitches dissolve on their own within one to two weeks.

If a wisdom tooth sits very close to a nerve or the sinus, we may take a 3D x-ray first, and we will tell you if we think a specialist is the better choice.

## What to expect on the day

1. **Review and x-rays.** We go over your health history and medications, especially blood thinners, and check the tooth on an x-ray.
2. **Numbing.** Numbing gel first, then local anesthetic. You will feel pressure during the extraction, but it should not be painful.
3. **Removal.** We take the tooth out and clean the area.
4. **Gauze and instructions.** You bite on gauze to help a blood clot form, and leave with written aftercare instructions.

Plan to take the rest of the day easy. For a surgical extraction, bring someone to drive you home if you feel unsure.

## Healing and aftercare

The blood clot that forms in the socket is the start of healing. Protecting it for the first few days is the most important thing you can do.

<ul class="check-list">
<li>Keep firm pressure on the gauze for 30 to 45 minutes</li>
<li>Do not spit, rinse hard, or drink through a straw for 24 hours</li>
<li>Avoid smoking and vaping for at least 72 hours</li>
<li>Use a cold pack on your cheek, 20 minutes on and 20 minutes off, on the first day</li>
<li>Eat soft, cool foods for a few days, and chew on the other side</li>
<li>After 24 hours, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals</li>
</ul>

Some soreness and swelling for two to three days is normal. Call us if pain gets worse after day three, if bleeding will not stop with gauze, or if you have a fever or a bad taste that does not go away. These can be signs of a dry socket or infection, and both are easy to treat.

## Cost and insurance

Costs vary depending on whether the extraction is simple or surgical, and on how many teeth are involved. Most PPO plans cover extractions for a share of the cost. We give you a written estimate before we start, and we can talk about replacing the tooth at the same time. See [insurance and financing](/insurance-and-financing).

## Common questions

<details class="faq">
<summary>Should I replace a tooth after it is removed?</summary>
<p>Wisdom teeth do not need replacing. For other teeth, a gap can let nearby teeth tip and shift over time. A <a href="/services/dental-implants">dental implant</a>, a bridge, or a partial denture can fill it, and we will walk you through each.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Do all wisdom teeth need to come out?</summary>
<p>No. If they have come in straight, you can clean them, and they are not causing problems, they can stay. We check them on x-rays as part of your regular exams.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>When can I go back to work?</summary>
<p>Most people go back the next day after a simple extraction. After wisdom teeth removal, plan on one to three days of rest.</p>
</details>
