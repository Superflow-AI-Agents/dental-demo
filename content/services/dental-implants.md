<p class="lede">A dental implant replaces the root of a missing tooth with a small titanium post set into the jawbone. Once the bone has grown around it, we attach a crown on top. The result is a tooth you brush and floss like any other, that does not move, and that can last for decades. We plan, place and restore implants in our own office, so you see the same team from first scan to final crown.</p>

## How an implant works

An implant has three parts:

<ul class="check-list">
<li><strong>The implant.</strong> A titanium post, about the size of a tooth root, placed in the bone.</li>
<li><strong>The abutment.</strong> A small connector that sits on the implant and rises just above the gum.</li>
<li><strong>The crown.</strong> The visible tooth, made to match the shade and shape of your other teeth.</li>
</ul>

Because the implant sits in bone, it carries chewing forces the way a natural root does. That helps keep the jawbone from shrinking, which it tends to do where a tooth is missing. Implants can also hold a bridge or anchor a full set of [dentures](/services/dentures) so they do not slip.

## Who is a good candidate

Most healthy adults who have lost a tooth can have an implant. What matters most:

<ul class="check-list">
<li>Enough bone in the jaw to hold the post, or a bone graft to build it up</li>
<li>Healthy gums, or gum disease treated first</li>
<li>No smoking, or a willingness to stop during healing, since smoking raises the risk of failure</li>
<li>Well-managed health conditions such as diabetes</li>
</ul>

Implants are not placed in children or young teens, because their jaws are still growing.

## What to expect, step by step

The full process usually takes three to six months, most of it quiet healing time.

1. **Consultation and 3D scan.** We examine the area, take a 3D x-ray to measure the bone and find nerves and sinuses, and talk through your options and costs.
2. **Extraction and grafting, if needed.** If the tooth is still there, we remove it. If bone is thin, we add a graft and let it heal first.
3. **Placing the implant.** With the area numb, we place the post in the bone and close the gum. Most single implants take about an hour.
4. **Healing.** Over the next few months, the bone bonds to the implant. You may wear a temporary tooth so there is no visible gap.
5. **The final crown.** We scan the implant, the lab makes your crown, and we attach it. Then you chew normally.

## Recovery and aftercare

Expect some swelling and tenderness for two to four days after placement. Cold packs and over-the-counter pain relief are enough for most people. Eat soft foods and chew on the other side for the first week or two.

Long term, care for an implant like a natural tooth: brush twice a day, floss or use an interdental brush around it daily, and keep up with [cleanings and exams](/services/cleanings-and-exams). An implant cannot decay, but the gum and bone around it can still get infected.

## Cost and insurance

Implant costs vary with the number of teeth, whether you need a graft, and the type of crown. Some PPO plans now cover part of an implant, and many cover the crown. We check your benefits and give you a written estimate for every stage before you decide. See [insurance and financing](/insurance-and-financing) for payment options.

## Common questions

<details class="faq">
<summary>Does getting an implant hurt?</summary>
<p>The area is fully numb during placement. Most people say the soreness afterward was less than they expected, often similar to having a tooth removed.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>How long do implants last?</summary>
<p>The implant itself can last for decades with good care. The crown on top sees daily wear and may need replacing after 10 to 15 years or more.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Can I have an implant if I lost the tooth years ago?</summary>
<p>Often, yes. The bone may have shrunk, so you may need a graft first. The 3D scan tells us exactly how much bone there is.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Is an implant better than a bridge?</summary>
<p>An implant leaves the neighboring teeth untouched and helps preserve bone. A bridge is faster and needs no surgery. We will help you weigh both. See <a href="/services/crowns-and-bridges">crowns and bridges</a>.</p>
</details>
