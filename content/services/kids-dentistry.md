## When to start, and how often

<ul class="check-list">
<li><strong>First visit:</strong> by your child's first birthday, or within six months of the first tooth, whichever comes first</li>
<li><strong>Regular checkups:</strong> every six months, or more often if your child gets cavities easily</li>
<li><strong>Around age 6:</strong> the first adult molars arrive, a good time for sealants</li>
<li><strong>Around age 7:</strong> a bite check, to spot crowding or jaw growth issues early</li>
<li><strong>Around age 12:</strong> the second molars arrive, and get sealants too</li>
</ul>

Baby teeth matter. They hold space for adult teeth, help with speech and chewing, and a cavity in one can hurt just as much as in an adult tooth.

## What happens at a kids visit

A first visit for a toddler is short and gentle. Often your child sits on your lap while we count teeth, check the gums, and show you how to brush. Older kids get a visit much like an adult's, in a smaller, friendlier format.

1. **Hello and a tour.** We show your child the chair, the light and the "tooth counter," and let them touch the tools first.
2. **Cleaning.** We brush and polish their teeth and floss between them. Many kids pick a polish flavor.
3. **X-rays, when needed.** Only when they help, using digital sensors with very low radiation.
4. **Exam.** Your dentist checks each tooth, the bite, and how teeth are coming in.
5. **Fluoride varnish.** A quick paint-on coating that helps strengthen enamel against cavities.
6. **Time for parents.** We tell you what we saw and answer questions about brushing, snacks, thumb sucking or teething.

Parents are welcome in the room for every visit.

## Preventing cavities

**Sealants** are thin, tooth-colored coatings painted into the grooves of back teeth, where most kids' cavities start. They take a few minutes per tooth, need no numbing, and can last for years.

At home, the basics make the biggest difference:

<ul class="check-list">
<li>Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste: a smear the size of a grain of rice under age 3, a pea-sized amount from 3 to 6</li>
<li>Help with brushing until about age 7 or 8, when most kids can do it well alone</li>
<li>Start flossing once teeth touch each other</li>
<li>Keep juice and sweet snacks to mealtimes, and give only water in bed</li>
</ul>

## When kids need treatment

If we find a cavity, we explain it to you first, then to your child in friendly terms. We use numbing gel before any injection and keep visits short. For a nervous child, we may split treatment into smaller visits. Kids get the same tooth-colored [fillings](/services/fillings) adults do.

If your child has a toothache, a chipped tooth or an injury, call us the same day. See [emergency dentistry](/services/emergency-dentistry).

## Common questions

<details class="faq">
<summary>My child is scared. What should I say before the visit?</summary>
<p>Keep it simple and positive: the dentist will count and clean their teeth. Avoid words like "hurt" or "shot," even to reassure. We have plenty of kid-friendly words of our own.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Is thumb sucking a problem?</summary>
<p>Most kids stop on their own by age 4, with no harm done. If it carries on as adult teeth come in, it can push teeth out of line. We will watch for that and suggest gentle ways to help.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Can my child see the same dentist as me?</summary>
<p>Yes. Many families book back-to-back visits so everyone is done in one trip.</p>
</details>
