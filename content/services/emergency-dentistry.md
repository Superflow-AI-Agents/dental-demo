## What counts as a dental emergency

If something hurts badly, is bleeding, or has broken, call us. You do not need to decide whether it is "bad enough." Common reasons people come in:

<ul class="check-list">
<li>A toothache that is severe, throbbing, or keeps you awake</li>
<li>Swelling in your gum, face or jaw</li>
<li>A cracked, chipped or broken tooth</li>
<li>A lost filling or a crown that has come off</li>
<li>Bleeding that will not stop after an extraction</li>
<li>A tooth that has been knocked loose or knocked out</li>
</ul>

A knocked-out adult tooth has the best chance of being saved in the first hour, so call immediately.

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>Go to a hospital emergency room right away</strong> if you have swelling that is spreading toward your eye or neck, trouble breathing or swallowing, a high fever with facial swelling, or a possible broken jaw. Those need care faster than a dental office can give.</p>
</div>

## What to do until you get here

<ul class="check-list">
<li><strong>Toothache:</strong> rinse with warm salt water and floss gently to clear any trapped food. An over-the-counter pain reliever taken as the label directs can help. Do not put aspirin on the gum, as it burns.</li>
<li><strong>Swelling:</strong> hold a cold pack on your cheek, 20 minutes on and 20 minutes off. Do not use heat.</li>
<li><strong>Broken tooth:</strong> rinse your mouth, save any pieces in a clean container, and avoid chewing on that side.</li>
<li><strong>Lost crown:</strong> keep it safe and bring it with you. Temporary dental cement from a pharmacy can hold it in place for a day.</li>
<li><strong>Bleeding:</strong> bite firmly on clean gauze or a damp tea bag for 30 minutes without checking.</li>
</ul>

## Same-day visits on weekdays

We keep time open every weekday for emergencies. Call the main line as early in the day as you can, and we will find you the first open slot. You can also book an emergency visit online and we will confirm by phone.

At your visit, we take an x-ray if needed, find the cause, and relieve the pain. Sometimes that means treating the tooth fully the same day. Other times we make you comfortable and book a follow-up for treatment such as a [root canal](/services/root-canals), a [crown](/services/crowns-and-bridges) or an [extraction](/services/extractions). Either way, you will know your options before we do anything.

## Common questions

<details class="faq">
<summary>Do I need to be a patient already?</summary>
<p>No. We see new patients for emergencies too. Bring a list of your medications and your insurance card if you have one.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>My toothache went away. Do I still need to come in?</summary>
<p>Yes. Pain that stops suddenly can mean the nerve inside the tooth has died, while infection keeps spreading. Book a visit soon so we can check.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Can you prescribe antibiotics over the phone?</summary>
<p>Antibiotics do not fix the cause of a dental infection, and we need to see the tooth to treat it safely. If you have swelling, we will get you in quickly.</p>
</details>
