## What crowns and bridges do

A crown is a cap that covers the whole visible part of a tooth. It holds a weakened tooth together and spreads the force of chewing so the tooth does not split. We usually recommend one for:

<ul class="check-list">
<li>A tooth with a large filling and little natural tooth left</li>
<li>A cracked tooth that hurts when you bite</li>
<li>A back tooth after a <a href="/services/root-canals">root canal</a></li>
<li>A worn, broken or badly discolored tooth</li>
</ul>

A bridge replaces one or more missing teeth. It is a row of crowns joined together: the crowns on each end fit over the teeth beside the gap, and the false tooth in the middle sits on the gum. A bridge is fixed in place, so you do not take it out. A [dental implant](/services/dental-implants) is the other common way to fill a gap, and we will talk through both.

## Choosing a crown material

There is no single best material. The right one depends on where the tooth is, how hard you bite, and whether you grind your teeth at night.

<div class="compare">

| Material | Looks | Strength | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| All-ceramic | Most natural | Good | Front teeth |
| Zirconia | Natural | Very strong | Most teeth |
| Porcelain fused to metal | Good | Strong | Bridges |
| Gold | Metal color | Strongest | Back molars |

</div>

Gold wears gently against the opposite teeth and needs the least trimming of your tooth, which is why some people still choose it for back molars. Porcelain fused to metal can show a thin gray line at the gum over time. We will show you samples and tell you what we would pick for our own mouths.

## What to expect, step by step

A crown usually takes two visits, about two weeks apart.

1. **Preparing the tooth.** After numbing, we remove any decay and shape the tooth so the crown can fit over it. For a bridge, we shape the teeth on both sides of the gap.
2. **Impressions and shade.** We take a digital scan or impression of your teeth and match the shade in natural light.
3. **A temporary crown.** You leave with a temporary crown that protects the tooth while the lab makes your final one.
4. **Fitting the final crown.** At the second visit, we remove the temporary, check the fit, shape and bite of the new crown, and cement it in place.

## Living with a crown or bridge

Avoid sticky or very hard foods while you have a temporary. If it comes loose, call us and keep it safe. Once the final crown is on, you can eat normally the same day.

Some tenderness in the gum and mild sensitivity to cold are normal for a few days. Clean around a crown like any other tooth. For a bridge, use a floss threader or small interdental brush to clean under the false tooth every day, because that is where plaque collects. With good care, crowns and bridges often last 10 to 15 years or longer.

## Cost and insurance

Costs vary with the material, the number of teeth involved, and whether the tooth needs other work first. Most PPO plans treat crowns and bridges as major care and cover part of the cost. ## Common questions

<details class="faq">
<summary>Does getting a crown hurt?</summary>
<p>The tooth is fully numb while we work, so most people feel pressure rather than pain. Expect some gum tenderness for a day or two afterward.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Can a crown get a cavity?</summary>
<p>The crown itself cannot decay, but the natural tooth underneath can, right at the edge where the crown meets the gum. Daily brushing and flossing there matters.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Should I get a bridge or an implant?</summary>
<p>An implant does not involve the teeth beside the gap, but it takes several months and needs enough bone. A bridge is faster but relies on trimming the neighboring teeth. If those teeth already have large fillings, a bridge can make good sense.</p>
</details>
