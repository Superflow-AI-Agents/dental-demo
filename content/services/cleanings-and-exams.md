## What happens at a visit

A routine visit takes about an hour. Your hygienist does most of it, and your dentist joins for the exam at the end.

1. **A quick health check.** We review medications and any changes since your last visit, and take your blood pressure.
2. **Gum measurements.** Your hygienist measures the small pocket around each tooth. Numbers of 1 to 3 millimeters are healthy. Deeper pockets can be an early sign of gum disease.
3. **Cleaning.** We remove hardened tartar with an ultrasonic scaler and hand tools, then polish and floss.
4. **X-rays, if they are due.** Most adults need bitewing x-rays once a year. We use digital sensors, which need a fraction of the radiation of film.
5. **Exam.** Your dentist checks each tooth, your bite, your gums and the soft tissue of your mouth, including an oral cancer screening.

## How often should you come in?

Every six months works for most people. If you have a history of gum disease, we may suggest every three or four months for a while. If your gums are very healthy and you have had no cavities for years, once a year can be enough. We will tell you which group you are in and why.

## Common questions

<details class="faq">
<summary>Does a cleaning hurt?</summary>
<p>For most people it feels like pressure and vibration, not pain. If your gums are inflamed they may be tender, and we can numb those areas first.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Why do my gums bleed when you clean them?</summary>
<p>Bleeding usually means inflammation from plaque along the gumline. It often settles within two weeks of daily flossing.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Can I skip x-rays?</summary>
<p>You can always decline. We will explain what x-rays would show and note your choice. Cavities between teeth are often invisible without them.</p>
</details>
