## How clear aligners work

Clear aligners are a series of custom plastic trays. Each one is shaped slightly differently from the last. Wearing them in order applies gentle, steady pressure that moves your teeth step by step toward the planned position.

They work well for mild to moderate crowding, small gaps, and many bite problems. Very complex cases, such as large jaw differences or teeth that need to rotate a long way, may do better with braces. We will tell you honestly if aligners are not the right tool for your teeth.

## Who they are for

Aligners suit adults and teens who:

<ul class="check-list">
<li>Have healthy teeth and gums, or will treat any cavities and gum disease first</li>
<li>Can wear the trays for 20 to 22 hours a day</li>
<li>Want an option that is easy to hide and take out for meals</li>
<li>Had braces as a kid and have seen their teeth shift back</li>
</ul>

The trays only work while they are in your mouth. If you know you will leave them in a drawer, braces may get you a better result.

## What to expect, step by step

1. **Consultation and scan.** We examine your teeth, take x-rays and photos, and make a digital 3D scan. No goopy impressions.
2. **Your treatment plan.** We map out how each tooth will move and show you a preview of the planned result on screen, along with a timeline.
3. **Attachments.** Many plans use small, tooth-colored bumps bonded to some teeth. They give the trays something to grip. They come off at the end.
4. **Wearing your trays.** You switch to a new set every one to two weeks. Each new set feels snug for a day or two.
5. **Check-ins.** We see you every six to ten weeks to check progress. Some people need a few extra trays near the end to fine-tune.
6. **Retainers.** Once your teeth are in place, you wear a retainer to keep them there.

Treatment often takes 6 to 18 months, depending on how far your teeth need to move.

## Daily life with aligners

Take your aligners out to eat and to drink anything except plain water. Brush your teeth before you put them back in, so food is not trapped against your teeth. Rinse the trays and brush them gently with cool water. Hot water can warp them.

Speech may feel a little different for the first few days. Any soreness from a new set usually fades in a day or two.

## Retainers matter

Teeth tend to drift back toward where they started, for years after treatment. A retainer is what keeps your result. Most people wear one every night at first, and then several nights a week for the long term. Skipping it is the most common reason teeth move again.

## Cost and insurance

Costs vary with how complex your case is and how long treatment takes. Some PPO plans include orthodontic benefits that cover part of aligner treatment, often with a lifetime limit. ## Common questions

<details class="faq">
<summary>Do aligners hurt?</summary>
<p>You will feel pressure and some tenderness for a day or two with each new set. That means they are working. Most people find it milder than braces.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Can I drink coffee with them in?</summary>
<p>It is best not to. Hot drinks can warp the trays, and coffee or sugary drinks can stain them and sit against your teeth. Take them out, drink, rinse, and put them back.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Are aligners right for my teenager?</summary>
<p>They can be, if your teen will wear them consistently. We often start kids with a bite check around age seven. See <a href="/services/kids-dentistry">kids dentistry</a>.</p>
</details>
