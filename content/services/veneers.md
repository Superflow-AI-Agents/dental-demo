## What veneers are

A veneer is a thin shell bonded to the front of a tooth. It covers the part people see when you smile and talk. Veneers are mostly used on the front six to ten upper teeth, and sometimes on the lower ones.

They can help with:

<ul class="check-list">
<li>Chipped, worn or uneven edges</li>
<li>Small gaps between teeth</li>
<li>Teeth that are slightly crooked or oddly shaped</li>
<li>Deep stains that do not respond to whitening</li>
</ul>

Veneers are not a fix for decay, gum disease, or a bite that is badly out of line. We treat those first. For crowding, [clear aligners](/services/clear-aligners) can often move teeth into place with no change to the enamel, and that may be all you need.

## Porcelain or composite?

**Porcelain veneers** are made by a dental lab. They resist stains, reflect light much like natural enamel, and often last 10 to 15 years or more. They usually take two visits and involve removing a thin layer of enamel, about half a millimeter, so the veneer sits flush.

**Composite veneers** are built directly on your teeth in one visit, using the same tooth-colored resin as a filling. They often need little or no enamel removed and cost less. They stain and chip more easily than porcelain and typically last five to seven years, but they can be repaired.

We will tell you which we think suits your teeth, and why.

## What to expect, step by step

For porcelain veneers:

1. **Consultation.** We examine your teeth and gums, take photos and a digital scan, and talk about what you want to change.
2. **Design and preview.** We plan the new shape and can place a temporary mock-up over your teeth so you can see and feel the result before any enamel is touched.
3. **Preparation.** After numbing, we remove a thin layer of enamel, scan the teeth, and fit temporary veneers.
4. **Bonding.** About two weeks later, we check the fit and shade of the final veneers, then bond them in place and adjust your bite.

## Caring for veneers

Brush twice a day with a non-abrasive toothpaste, floss daily, and keep up with your [cleanings and exams](/services/cleanings-and-exams). Veneers are strong but not unbreakable. Do not use your teeth to open packages, and avoid biting hard things like ice or pen caps. If you grind your teeth at night, wear a night guard to protect them.

The teeth underneath can still decay at the edges, so regular checkups still matter.

## Cost and insurance

Costs vary with the number of teeth and the material you choose. Because veneers are usually cosmetic, dental insurance rarely covers them. If a veneer also repairs a broken tooth, part of the cost may be covered. We give you a written estimate before any work begins. See [insurance and financing](/insurance-and-financing) for payment options.

## Common questions

<details class="faq">
<summary>Are veneers permanent?</summary>
<p>Porcelain veneers involve removing a small amount of enamel, which does not grow back. That tooth will always need a veneer or crown. That is why we only suggest them when they are clearly the right option.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Should I whiten my teeth first?</summary>
<p>If you want your other teeth lighter too, yes. Veneers do not change color, so we match them to your teeth after <a href="/services/teeth-whitening">whitening</a>.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Will veneers look fake?</summary>
<p>Not when they are planned well. We pick a shade and shape that suit your face and your other teeth, and you see a preview first. Teeth that are too white or too even are what look artificial.</p>
</details>
