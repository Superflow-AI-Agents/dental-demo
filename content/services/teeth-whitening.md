## How whitening works

Teeth darken over time as coffee, tea, red wine and tobacco leave stains, and as the enamel thins and the darker layer underneath shows through. Whitening gel contains peroxide, which breaks down stain molecules inside the enamel. It does not remove or damage the enamel when used as directed.

Whitening works best on yellow or brown staining. Gray staining, from certain medicines taken in childhood or from a tooth that has been injured, responds less well. Crowns, veneers and fillings do not change color at all, so if you have them on front teeth, we will plan around them.

Before whitening, we make sure there are no cavities, exposed roots or gum problems. Gel on an untreated cavity can be painful. If you are not a current patient, start with an exam.

## What to expect

1. **Check and shade.** We examine your teeth and record your starting shade, so we can measure the change.
2. **Cleaning, if due.** Whitening works better on clean teeth. We often pair it with a [cleaning](/services/cleanings-and-exams).
3. **Whitening.** In the office, or at home with your trays and written instructions.
4. **Follow-up.** We check the result and talk about how to keep it.

## Sensitivity, honestly

Sensitivity is the most common side effect of whitening. Many people feel some, usually as short twinges or a dull ache, especially with cold drinks. It is temporary and normally fades within a day or two of stopping.

To keep it manageable:

<ul class="check-list">
<li>Brush with a toothpaste for sensitive teeth for two weeks before and during whitening</li>
<li>With trays, use them every other day, or for shorter sessions, if your teeth feel tender</li>
<li>Tell us if you already have sensitive teeth, so we can adjust the gel strength</li>
</ul>

If sensitivity is a big worry for you, take-home trays give you more control than a single in-office session.

## Keeping your results

Results last from several months to a few years, depending on what you eat and drink. For the first 48 hours, while teeth pick up stain more easily, go easy on coffee, tea, red wine and dark sauces. After that, rinse with water after staining drinks, keep up with cleanings, and use your trays for a short touch-up when you notice fading.

Whitening is cosmetic, so dental insurance does not usually cover it. See [insurance and financing](/insurance-and-financing) for payment options.

## Common questions

<details class="faq">
<summary>Is whitening safe for my enamel?</summary>
<p>Yes, when it is supervised and used as directed. Peroxide gels have been studied for decades. Overusing strong gel, or using it on untreated cavities, is what causes problems.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>How white will my teeth get?</summary>
<p>It depends on your starting shade and the type of stain. Most people see their teeth lighten by several shades. We will give you a realistic idea at your first check, not a promise.</p>
</details>

<details class="faq">
<summary>Can I whiten if I have veneers or crowns?</summary>
<p>You can whiten your natural teeth, but the crowns and veneers will not change. If you plan new <a href="/services/veneers">veneers</a> or crowns, whiten first, then we match them to your new shade.</p>
</details>
